MLB Pitcher Arsenal Analysis ~ Russell Petersen

This project uses 2025 MLB data of 125 starting pitchers from the website Baseball Savant to investigate how a pitcher's pitch arsenal — the types and mix of pitches they throw — affects key performance metrics. Many Major League Pitchers are said to be one adjustment or one pitch away from being one of the best in the league and this project takes a deep dive into if that claim really holds true.

Pitcher Categories

To better understand how different pitching styles impact performance, pitchers were categorized into distinct groups based on their primary pitch types and usage percentages. This process involved analyzing the proportion of each pitch thrown, such as four-seam fastballs, sinkers, changeups, and breaking balls. Each pitcher was assessed against a set of predefined thresholds, which identified their most frequently used pitches and their overall arsenal composition.

Pitchers were then assigned to a group, with a specific order of evaluation to ensure accurate classification. For instance, pitchers demonstrating high usage of a particular off-speed pitch, like a changeup, were first identified as 'Changeup Specialists'. Others were grouped based on combinations of their fastball types with secondary pitches, such as 'Four Seam + Sweeper' or 'Cutter/Sinker'. Pitchers with a diverse mix of several pitches, none dominating, were categorized into a 'Big Arsenal' group, ensuring that each pitcher found their most fitting category within the defined pitching styles.

Results

This is how each pitch arsenal performed across key statistical categories: Expected Weighted On-Base Average (xWOBA), Strikeout Minus Walk Percentage (K-BB%), Whiff Percentage (Whiff%), and Average Exit Velocity. **xWOBA** measures the quality of contact a pitcher allows; a lower xWOBA indicates they are limiting hitters to weaker outcomes. **K-BB%** reflects a pitcher's command and swing-and-miss ability, with higher percentages being more desirable as they show a greater ability to strike out batters while minimizing walks. **Whiff%** directly quantifies how often batters swing and miss at pitches, highlighting a pitcher's raw stuff and ability to generate swings and misses. Finally, **Average Exit Velocity** indicates the average speed of batted balls against a pitcher; lower values suggest they induce weaker contact. By analyzing these metrics together, we can identify unique performance profiles for each pitcher group, revealing their strengths and weaknesses and helping to understand their overall effectiveness.
